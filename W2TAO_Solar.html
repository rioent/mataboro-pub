Reading the numbers

Approximate HF operating guides, not band-opening forecasts. DRAP ranges do not define NOAA radio-blackout levels. Read these numbers together with path, time of day, MUF, and on-air conditions.

DRAP

ReadingPractical meaning
0–2 MHzLower absorption indicator. Low bands can still have daytime absorption.
2–4 MHz160/80 m may suffer on affected paths.
4–7 MHzGreater risk of absorption on 80/60/40 m.
7–10 MHz40 m and lower bands may be weak; examine your path.
10–15 MHzAbsorption can extend into 30/20 m on affected paths.
15+ MHzHigher HF frequencies can also be affected. Check the DRAP map.

Higher means absorption reaches higher frequencies. A global maximum may be far from your radio path.

Planetary Kp

ReadingPractical meaning
0–2Quiet. Usually favors stable HF paths.
3Unsettled. Northern paths may vary.
4Active. More fading and path changes possible.
5 · G1Minor geomagnetic storm.
6 · G2Moderate geomagnetic storm.
7 · G3Strong geomagnetic storm.
8 · G4Severe geomagnetic storm.
9 · G5Extreme geomagnetic storm.

High Kp often disrupts high-latitude HF paths. Auroral VHF opportunities may appear.

Solar flux

ReadingPractical meaning
Below 75Low activity. Upper HF F2 openings often limited.
75–10020 m often useful; upper bands more variable.
100–130More potential on 17/15 m and sometimes 10 m.
130–160Good upper HF potential when the field is quiet.
160–200Strong upper HF potential, subject to path and season.
200+High activity. High MUFs possible; check storm conditions.

These ranges are informal guides. Solar flux measures radio emissions, a proxy for solar activity, not a direct measurement of your path's ionization.
